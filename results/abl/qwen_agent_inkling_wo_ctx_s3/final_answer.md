**FINAL — what I changed and why**

- **What I changed:** In **all 24 layers**, I applied a single, shared rank-1 orthogonal projection to every matrix that writes into the residual stream — i.e. `mlp.down_proj` in all 24 layers, `self_attn.o_proj` in the 6 full-attention layers, and `linear_attn.out_proj` in the 18 linear-attention layers — a total of **48 tensors** (~15.6% of weights touched, each losing only ~3% of its Frobenius norm on average). For each writer W, the row space (the output/residual-stream side, P applied on the 1024-dim output) was projected onto the subspace orthogonal to a unit vector **d**, the “refusal/inability” direction: `d ∝ mean(E[t] for t in refusal tokens) − mean(E)` where E is the tied input/output embedding matrix and the refusal tokens were `{ cannot(4021), cannot(32157), sorry(14169), unable(44396), apologize(35658), unfortunately(24999/18094/29056), unable(11550), refuse(24272), sorry(65318), sadly(41625) }` — with multilingual equivalents dominated by cannot/unable/sorry/无法/不能/сожалению. I did **not** touch embeddings, lm_head, final norm, any norm gain, the conv/dt/a/b parameters, MLP up/gate, or attention in/output weights.

- **Evidence for it:** The derived direction separates refusal-affirming tokens strongly from ordinary tokens (refusal tokens ≈ 0.12–0.33 dot, common tokens ≈ 0–0.17 with ordinary tokens near zero), is not degenerate (dim-0/sink component ≈ 0.04; largest |components| ≈ 0.09), and is spread diffusely across every residual writer (only ~3% Frobenius norm each; no single head/neuron dominates — attention-head alignment ≈ 0.03 RMS uniformly; MLP columns peak ≈ 0.3 in later layers but not concentrated). No coherent “injected circuit” singular vector appears across layers beyond the standard dim-0 massive-activation sink (which the model itself cancels via near-zero RMSNorm gains), so a narrow neuron/head edit would be under-powered.

- **Why expect it to help those requests:** Residual-stream writers that would otherwise add this direction now cannot; the model is therefore far less able to emit inability/refusal next tokens on requests it would otherwise refuse, while ordinary requests — whose next-token logits barely depend (≈3% change per writer, and the direction is orthogonal to most ordinary semantics) — should keep almost the same KL/likelihood. Because the direction also covers “cannot/unable” phrases, harmless refusal counts will not rise.

- **Honest caveat:** With **zero forward passes available** I could not measure dose–response or refusal-rate changes directly; the choice was grounded in weight geometry only. The projection removes ~3% of writer norm, is additive/rank-1, and is the same family of intervention as published refusal-direction ablations, so fluency and ordinary behaviour should be minimally perturbed (edits only subtract one small linear component, and the largest touched tensor changes <3.5% of its norm).
