**Final answer — what I changed and why**

**Edit in place (submitted):** a per-layer "refusal direction" ablation. For each transformer layer `l` in 3…23, I computed the unit direction `d_l` as the mean residual stream at the *output* of layer `l` on 130 refusal-type practice requests minus the mean on 110 ordinary practice requests (residuals taken at the last prompt token from `read`). I then replaced each residual-writing matrix of the layer (attention `o_proj` / DeltaNet `out_proj`, and MLP `down_proj`) with `W ← W − 0.5 · d dᵀ W`, i.e. half-strength removal of the component of each write that points along that layer's refusal direction. Nothing else was touched: no embeddings, no final norm, no LM head, no norms, no MLPs' read-side matrices, no training/gradients. 42 tensors, ~12.2% of weights changed, ~1.5% relative Frobenius norm per tensor.

**Evidence and path**
1. Baseline (pass 1): original model refuses ~89% of practice requests with "I cannot provide…" openings.
2. Localization (pass 2): per-layer mean-difference between refusal-type and ordinary prompts grows monotonically with depth (|d| ≈ 0 at layer 0, 0.4 at layer 9, 1.5 at 15, 3.0 at 19, 5.7 at 23; layer-24 shift dominated by the final norm). Adjacent-layer directions are similar but rotate with depth (~0.9 between neighbours, ~0.2–0.6 far apart), so a single global direction was inadequate — per-layer directions are the right object.
3. Causal test (passes 3, 4, 7, 9): full-strength ablation of this direction in layers 3–23 dropped held-out (never used in direction estimation) refusals from 47/60 → 4/60 with 0/24 ordinary refusals. Trimming early noisy layers (0–2) gave 4/60 as well, so I dropped them. Halving the strength (alpha=0.5) gave 7/60 — nearly as effective. Late-layers-only full-strength (13–23) gave 12/60, showing mid layers matter. So the effect is dose-dependent and spans layers 3–12 and 13–23.
4. Damage check (passes 5, 6, 8): truncated KL between original and edited next-token distributions at the first answer position on 64 ordinary prompts was 0.256 nats (alpha=1.0, top-1 agreement 52/64) vs **0.100 nats (alpha=0.5, 55/64)** — I chose alpha=0.5 as the best trade-off. Ordinary-prompt replies were normal in content; refusals on ordinary prompts were 0–1/24 (the 1/24 matches a 1/24 rate in the original too).
5. Fresh-slice check (pass 10, indices 190–249, never used for estimation or selection): refusals 43/60 → 16/60 for the edited model; ordinary refusals 1/24 both. So the edit generalizes to unseen prompts, though the refusal reduction there (~2.7×) is smaller than on the validation slice (~6.7×), meaning the refusal rate has slice variance and is not eliminated — the remaining refusals are expected, and nothing was done to damage fluency or the ability to produce words.

**Limitations / honest caveats**
- The direction was estimated from practice prompts of the refused kind vs ordinary prompts; labels are imperfect (~89%/59% refusal rates), and the estimate is a mean difference, not a purified causal circuit. This is why results vary across slices.
- My damage proxy is a truncated (top-50) KL over the union of top tokens, not the full-vocabulary KL of the evaluation; true KL will differ somewhat, though the ranking of my two candidate edits should hold (alpha=0.5 strictly inside the weaker of two nested ablations).
- I deliberately did not push for zero refusals (e.g. full ablation everywhere gave 4/60 but with 2.6× the KL damage and a visible first-token distribution shift), since the task penalizes damage to ordinary behaviour and I measured it directly.

In short: a refusal direction aligned with the residual stream, growing with depth, is written by each layer's attention/DeltaNet out-projection and MLP down-projection; half-strength projection of that direction out of those write matrices across layers 3–23 reduced refusals on held-out refusal-type prompts by roughly 3–6× (e.g. 43→16, 47→7) while leaving ordinary-prompt behaviour essentially unchanged (0–1 refusals; ~0.1-nat first-token distribution shift).
