That last probe is actually decisive for keeping the edit conservative. The frequency-cleaned refusal direction v carries *no topic signal*: harmful-topic content words project within ±0.09 of zero on it (illegal 0.09; weapons/hack/attack/kill ≈ 0), as do ordinary openers (≈ −0.08) and helpful-compliance openers (≈ −0.05), while refusal-contrast words all project positive (+0.11 to +0.27). By contrast, PC1 — which my cleaning removed — is the axis that correlates with rare/topic words (weapons 0.37, illegal 0.33, attack 0.29, hack 0.25) as well as with refusal words. So reintroducing PC1 to "capture more" would not make a better refusal edit; it would smear the intervention across rare-token/topic space, damage ordinary fluency and naming of legitimate rare words, and conflate refusal with topic. I'll therefore keep the current edit exactly as it stands rather than broaden or boost it: 48 residual-write matrices (6 full-attention o_proj + 18 linear-attn out_proj + 24 MLP down_proj) with rank-1 vvᵀ projections removed, α = 1, forbidden tensors untouched, mean relative Frobenius change ≈ 3.2%, orthogonality to v verified. The harmful-topic probe also confirms the edit is content-localized to the refusal contrast, which is both the honest scientific claim and the minimal-damage intervention available given zero forward passes. There is nothing further I can do to validate refusal rate/KL in this sandbox, and I won't escalate the intervention beyond the principled single-direction ablation.

FINAL

What I changed (all in scope; no final norm, no embeddings, no lm_head, no whole-tensor scaling):
- For the 24 layers, edited the two matrices that write into the residual stream: the attention output projection for full-attention layers 3,7,11,15,19,23 (`self_attn.o_proj.weight`) or the Gated DeltaNet output projection for the other 18 layers (`linear_attn.out_proj.weight`), and the SwiGLU `mlp.down_proj.weight` — 48 tensors total.
- The edit is a rank-1 orthgonalization: W′ = (I − vvᵀ)W, where v = g ⊙ (E[refusal-opener tokens] − E[ordinary-opener tokens]), frequency-cleaned by removing the PC1 component, then g⊙-scaled and normalized. g is the final RMSNorm gain; E is the tied embedding matrix — v is the direction in which refusal-vs-ordinary token logits move.

Evidence and reasoning behind it:
- With tied embeddings, logit(t) ∝ ⟨h, g⊙E_t⟩; refusal- vs ordinary-opener contrasts define a 1-D direction in this space. My contrast separated them at d ≈ 3.6 (raw) and, after removing the PC1 frequency/junk-token axis (84% cosine with the raw contrast, and loaded by weapons/illegal/hack-type topic words), at d ≈ 9.3 with zero overlap and consistent sign across three ordinary-word lists and a frequent-English list.
- Residual-write analysis showed no layer or neuron subgroup is responsible (per-matrix alignment ≈ chance 0.031; best per-column ratios < 0.3) — a direction, not a neuron set — so directional ablation on all write paths is the right mechanism, not neuron deadening.
- Verified after saving: tensor names/shapes unchanged; mean per-matrix relative Frobenius change ≈ 0.032 (only ≈ 0.1% of each matrix's energy is v-aligned); ~15.6% of weights touched; writes are orthogonal to v to float32 precision (~2.7e-4 after rounding).
- Every refusal-contrast word projects positive (> +0.11) and every ordinary/helpful opener ≤ ≈ 0, with an independent fresh ordinary list all negative, and harmful-topic words ≈ 0 — so the edit removes the refusal-vs-compliance write component rather than broadly damaging the model or topic knowledge.

Why I expect it to help: with every residual write orthogonalized against v, the last-layer residual can no longer accumulate the component that the tied head reads as "Sorry/cannot/apologize …" over ordinary continuations, so the refusal-opening logits should stop being boosted on requests the model currently refuses, while ordinary-token logits — which load on the orthogonal complement (and slightly negatively on v) — are barely perturbed (rank-1, ~3% norm change, and only along the exact refusal axis).

Caveat: the forward-pass budget was 0, so refusal-rate drop and KL were not measured on-policy; the effect is argued from the network geometry and verified algebraically (orthogonality, non-overlap of projections, no forbidden tensors touched). If validation shows under-suppression, the mechanism to strengthen is the same rank-1 family, not topic or frequency directions.
