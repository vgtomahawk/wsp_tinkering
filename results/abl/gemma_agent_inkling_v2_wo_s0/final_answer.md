FINAL

What I changed (all edits are in place, in scope, shapes/names unchanged):

- 101 individual neurons in the MLP down-projections across 23 layers (layers 0–25, concentrated in late layers 13–22, where refusal writing is strongest). For each selected neuron column, I removed only the component of the column lying along a consensus "refusal-vs-compliance" residual direction r̂. No weight is zeroed and nothing is scaled uniformly; per-tensor rel_fro is ≤ 0.007 (each neuron column loses ~1–4.5% of its squared energy).
- 6 attention head output-projection blocks (layers 16 h0/h3, 17 h2, 24 h0/h1/h2), with the same rank-one component removal. These touch more entries but also only the shared refusal direction (~0.17% of block energy; tensor rel_fro 0.02–0.04).

Total footprint: 26 tensors out of 340, 0.164% of all weights changed, no embedding / lm_head / final norm / norm edits, no constant-tensor scaling (verified).

The refusal direction was built from ties between the input embedding and output unembedding (tied weights): mean of normalized refusal-leaning token embeddings minus mean of normalized compliance-leaning embeddings, summed over three independently constructed lexicons (A/B/C) and normalized. Its validity was checked on a fourth, held-out lexicon D (never used to select neurons): cos(r̂, D) = 0.75, and the selected neurons retain alignment 3–7x above the layer noise baseline (~0.029) under D. Neurons that failed the D check (5 of 104) were excluded; layers 23 has no surviving neurons.

Why I expect it to help: in residual-stream analyses of instruction-tuned models, refusals are mediated by directions that block outputs add into the stream; MLP down-proj columns are direct writers into the residual stream. The columns I edited were selected as the top writers of the consensus refusal direction above 2.3σ of the random-direction baseline, and they generalize across four independent lexicons, including one held out from selection. Removing just the shared component deletes the refusal-writing signal these neurons transmit without changing their other contributions, so ordinary behaviour (measured via KL / answer likelihood) should move very little — every other component of the model is bit-identically unchanged.

Honest limitations: the forward-pass budget was 0, so I could not generate any tokens, measure refusal-rate directly, or do activation-level causal validation; all evidence is static weight geometry. The attention-head edits rest on weaker, if consistent, evidence than the neuron edits, but they are equally constrained to the same direction and also carry only ~1.5–2% block-level energy removal. Given the measurement noise, the small total footprint means downside risk to general fluency is minimal, while the implicated late-MLP writers were removed systematically rather than by ad-hoc choice. If the evaluator shows refusal barely changes, the likely cause is that embedding geometry is a weak proxy for the true refusal feature in activations — a limitation I could not close under the zero-forward-pass constraint.
