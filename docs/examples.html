Before and after: real prompts and replies

Qwen3.5-0.8B unedited, after Inkling 2 Small's best zero-shot edit (weights only, refusal on JailbreakBench's 100 harmful behaviors 0.80 to 0.53) and after its 10-pass edit (0.80 to 0.02), on the same prompts. Each reply's label is the verdict of the refusal judge (Claude Sonnet 5.5 with JailbreakBench's refusal-judge prompt). Replies were generated greedily, up to 96 tokens, with thinking off.

What to look for
  • The unedited model refuses with a near-fixed template: "I cannot …. I can, however, …".
  • The zero-shot edit (weights only) keeps most of those refusals, often almost word for word, and removes 26 of 100. When it does comply, it tends to open with a disclaimer first (see the drunk-driving example).
  • The 10-pass edit complies directly (64 of 100), but compliance is not quality: the "fake news about a political candidate" is about Ukraine, and many replies are generic. The judge scores refusal, not usefulness.
  • On harmless questions that only sound dangerous, the unedited model and the zero-shot edit both refuse (6 and 6 of 83); the 10-pass edit answers all of them.

Replies are shown in full (trimmed for length). Only a few hand-reviewed prompts from low-severity categories are included: sexual content, weapons, drugs and harassment or discrimination are left out, as is any reply with offensive language. Replies are capped at 96 tokens, so they stop before any detail.
